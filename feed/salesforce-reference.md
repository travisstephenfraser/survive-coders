# Salesforce Park and Tower: reference notes

Gathered from web search on 2026-09-27 for the Park and Tower levels. Photo downloads were
blocked by the session's network policy, so these are written facts only. Swap in real photos
for the art pass when you have them.

## Salesforce Transit Center (Level 1's exit)
- Pearlescent white exterior wall: 3,992 perforated white aluminum panels.
- The perforations follow a Roger Penrose rhombus tiling; about 37% of the surface is open.
- Architect: Pelli Clarke Pelli.

## Salesforce Park (the roof of the Transit Center)
- 5.4 acres, about 70 ft above the street, open to the sky.
- 600 trees and 16,000 plants in 13 botanical zones, each with an interpretive sign.
- A half-mile walking loop runs around the perimeter, lined with benches and lawns.
- **Bus Fountain** by Ned Kahn: 247 geysers along about 1,200 ft. Motion sensors in the ceiling
  of the bus deck below set the frequency, motion and height of the water as buses come and go.
  (In the game, each "bus" fires the jets in a wave.)
- **Amphitheater Lawn and Stage** at the west end; room for more than 1,000 people.
- Children's play area between the central lawn and the main plaza (a climbing rope frame on
  spongy flooring), plus a cafe.
- Access by elevator, escalator, stairs, skybridge, or a free 20-passenger aerial tram
  ("gondola"). The tram runs one way, from Mission St & Fremont St up to the park.

## Salesforce Tower (415 Mission St)
- Crown: Jim Campbell's *Day for Night*, 11,000 LEDs across the top six floors showing
  low-resolution moving images. The LEDs point back at the building and bounce off the crown's
  perforated aluminum panels, so the images are visible up to about 20 miles away.
- The 61st floor is the **Ohana Floor**, an observation deck and lounge for employees and
  guests, with room for about 250 people.
